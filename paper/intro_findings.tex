\paragraph{What we found.}
With one verdict per training row, one on-policy pass improves Vega on DecisionBench by $\gainSmallPkOneDb$ points at 0.8B and $\gainBigPkOneDb$ at 4B. Reusing a single logged sampled verdict per row for eight optimisation passes gives $\gainSmallLogIpsDb$ and $\gainBigLogIpsDb$. Eight passes that collect fresh verdicts give $\gainSmallPkDb$ and $\gainBigPkDb$; the logged arm remains $\absSmallLogIpsDbVsPk$ points behind at 0.8B, a difference that survives Holm adjustment. This comparison is consistent with a benefit from additional feedback, but does not isolate information from the change in collection policy and off-policy objective. Repeated verdicts are particularly informative: on DecisionBench each row produces $\revealsSmallPkDb$ correct-answer events on average at 0.8B, each identifying its label. Eight-pass feedback therefore approaches the labelled adapter's point estimates on most Vega benchmarks without establishing equivalence. A per-task label prior recovers much of the gain on typed-decisions and the public menu; DecisionBench and emotion provide clearer evidence of improvement beyond prior correction. On Laya, encoder training with verdicts improves the released model, but matched labelled training wins on typed-decisions and emotion by $\absLayaPkEncTypedVsSupEnc$ and $\absLayaPkEncEmoVsSupEnc$ points. These two encoder comparisons have one seed each. The calibration, KL and switch controls identify useful components and failure modes, rather than guarantees of deployment safety. All one-rating descriptions concern training rows; calibration and model selection use additional held-out feedback.
