\paragraph{What we found.}
Under eight passes, verdicts come close to labels on Vega: the feedback-trained adapter is within about two points of the same adapter trained on labels and early-stopped on every benchmark except emotion ($\gainSmallPkEmoVsSupEs$ and $\gainBigPkEmoVsSupEs$, the only gaps that survive Holm adjustment). This is less remarkable than it sounds: on DecisionBench each training row produced $\revealsSmallPkDb$ verdicts of ``right'' on average, each of which names the gold answer, so the two arms were mostly training on the same labels with different weights. With one rating per row the gain falls. One on-policy pass gives $\gainSmallPkOneDb$ and $\gainBigPkOneDb$ on DecisionBench against $\gainSmallPkDb$ and $\gainBigPkDb$ with eight. One verdict per row on the released model's sampled answers, logged and trained to convergence, gives $\gainSmallLogIpsDb$ and $\gainBigLogIpsDb$: optimisation recovers part of the shortfall, but at 0.8B the remainder, $\absSmallLogIpsDbVsPk$ points, is significant, so on DecisionBench extra verdicts per row carry real information. Logging the released model's best answers instead of samples is neither clearly better nor clearly worse. Every one-rating arm is below the early-stopped labels except logged best answers on typed-decisions at 4B, which are level ($\gainBigLogArgTypedVsSupEs$). Against VegaML's TTT head under its current selection rule, the feedback-trained adapter is ahead on DecisionBench, where the significant margin sits on the rows the head leaves to the engine, level on the public menu and typed-decisions, and behind on emotion. On typed-decisions and the public menu a per-task label prior with no input features recovers most of the gain from the same verdicts; on DecisionBench and emotion it does not. On Laya, the matched labelled fine-tune with the encoder unfrozen beats the verdict-trained one by $\absLayaPkEncTypedVsSupEnc$ points on typed-decisions and $\absLayaPkEncEmoVsSupEnc$ on emotion and is level elsewhere; the first version's ``beats Laya's own fine-tune'' compared in-domain training with a transfer model. The temperature refit is needed for the probabilities and is top-label temperature scaling; the KL term is decisive in one cell; the switch, now comparing best answers, turns the add-on off where it hurts most and is noisy on small calibration sets. With Vega's shipped typed-decisions adapter switched off, so that the released model starts weak on that benchmark, labels beat verdicts by $\absSmallPkNoshipTypedVsSupNoship$ points at 0.8B, the clearest picture of what verdicts on sampled answers cannot do when the model rarely samples the right one.
